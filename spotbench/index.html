How a stream is scored

    
Streaming protocol and Timeliness-F1 (from the paper and evals/ in the SPOT-Bench GitHub repository)
    

      
The model watches the whole video as a stream, at 1 Hz in the reported results, and decides at every step whether to respond. Nothing pauses the video at the question. Every response it emits is scored, so a response at the wrong moment costs precision and a missed moment costs recall.

      
    
        
  1. Slots: each ground-truth response time t becomes a slot window [t − offset, t + offset]. A prediction inside the window gets Timeliness 1.
    2. 
        
  2. Timeliness outside the window decays as a Gaussian: σearly before the window, σlate after it, with σearly = 2σlate (anticipating is penalised less than lagging). Below 0.05 a prediction cannot match. The curves on the timeline are these functions.
    3. 
        
  3. Content check: ABD must say a start word (start, begin, ...) or an end word (end, finish, stop, ...), never both. PNR must say "now". SQA, SPG, SI and UI are judged by gpt-5-mini on a 0–5 scale and must reach the task threshold (3 for SQA and SPG, 2 for SI and UI). "No", "none" and similar replies count as silence.
    4. 
        
  4. Matching: predictions are taken earliest first and matched greedily to open slots. A slot absorbs at most K = 5 predictions and keeps its best Timeliness. Unmatched predictions are false positives; unmatched slots are false negatives.
    5. 
        
  5. Metrics: T-Score = mean best Timeliness over all slots. Timeliness-F1: precision = ΣT / (matched slots + FP), recall = ΣT / (matched slots + FN), F1 = their harmonic mean. Reported as T-Score@5 / T-F1@5.
    6. 
        
  6. Model input: ABD, PNR and SQA questions arrive at their ask_time. SPG and SI give the task and its ordered step list at 0 s, so the challenge is timing. UI gives only a standing instruction to warn about safety-critical events.
    7.
